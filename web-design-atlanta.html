My approach

Strategy first, design second

A beautiful website with no strategy behind it is a billboard in the desert. Before I open a design tool, I want to know who your customers are, what they search for, and what you want a visitor to do when they land on your page. The design then serves that goal, not the other way around.

Every project follows the same simple path, so you always know what comes next:

1. Discovery

We talk about your business, your services, your ideal customer and the pages you actually need. No extra pages just to fill space.

2. Structure & copy

I map the pages, plan the headings around what people search for, and write clear copy that explains what you do, who it's for and how to contact you.

3. Design & build

A clean, on-brand design built to work on phones first, then tablet and desktop, with fast loading in mind.

4. Launch & setup

Titles, descriptions, schema, analytics and Google Search Console are set up before launch so Google can find and understand the site from day one.

What a good small-business website needs

Five things every Atlanta business site should do

Whether you run a cleaning company in Marietta or a med spa in Milton, the websites that bring in customers tend to get the same basics right:

  • Say what you do and where, in the first screen
  • Make the next step obvious: call, book or message
  • Load fast and read easily on a phone
  • Have one clear page for each service you sell
  • Be set up so Google can read, index and understand it

Most small-business sites don't struggle because of bad design. They struggle because one of these five is missing, usually the clear next step or a page for each service.

Local SEO

Built to be found on Google, not just to look good

For a local business, being found means showing up when someone nearby searches for what you offer. I build that into the site itself:

  • Page titles and descriptions written for real searches
  • Clear headings and one topic per page
  • Local business and service schema markup
  • Sitemap submitted to Google Search Console and Bing
  • Descriptive image names and alt text
  • Internal links between your related pages

SEO is not a switch. It builds over weeks and months, and no one can honestly promise a ranking. What I can do is make sure your site has the right foundation and that nothing technical is holding it back.

What does a website need to show up on Google in Atlanta?

It needs a clear page for each service, titles and descriptions that match what people search for, fast loading on mobile, local business schema, and a sitemap submitted to Google Search Console.
